Reading Market Structure

Before trading an interest rate differential, confirm the trend on the price chart itself. Higher highs and higher lows confirm an uptrend — a break into a lower low is often the first sign the structure is shifting.

Interest Differential

Interest Differential is the difference between two interest rates. For example: USD 4.00% - CHF 0.00% = 4.00% Differential. Couple two currencies with different interest rates. It is always positive for the one with the higher rate.

The above image shows the central banks/commitment of traders/commercials (the red line). NB: Just because it is above zero basis line does not mean it is a buy, or below zero basis line means it will go down/sell.

What we are looking for:

Highest Interest Differential between currencies. Example: United States and Switzerland with 4.00% Differential.
Example: USD 4.00% - CHF 0.00% = 4.00%

Net long/Above zero basis line by commercials or Net short/below zero basis line by commercials for individual coupled currencies.

To come up with quantitative analysis or buy/sell program, check that coupled currencies have opposite sides from zero basis line.

Conclusion: USD is bullish if USD is above zero basis line and CHF is below zero basis line because USD has a higher interest rate against a weak CHF interest rate.

Then analyze the individual chart to find market structure break.

Below Zero Basis Line

Commercials are saying the currency is overbought and price is likely to go down over time.

Above Zero Basis Line

Commercials are saying the currency is oversold and price is likely to go up over time.

On interest charts, you will see small upward movements on a single currency bearish trend. That shows that smart money is hedging, basically taking profits from their short sells previously. And vice versa.

Note: The interest rate is not the only factor that determines the price of a currency. Other factors such as economic data, geopolitical events, and market sentiment also play a significant role.
